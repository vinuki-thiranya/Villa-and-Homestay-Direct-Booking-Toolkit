Reviews do two jobs for a small property. They persuade new guests to choose you, and they tell you what to fix. Most happy guests simply forget to write one. This guide shows how to ask at the right moment, in the right way, and within the rules each platform sets.

## Where reviews matter

- **Google** reviews appear next to your Business Profile on Search and Maps, which is where people who search your name will see them first.
- **Booking.com and Airbnb** reviews shape how guests choose between listings on those platforms.
- **Your own website** can show reviews from guests who booked direct.

You do not need to chase reviews on every platform equally. The OTAs run their own review systems; Airbnb, for example, [prompts guests on the day of check-out](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/13). Google is where your own request makes the most difference, because nobody else is asking for you.

## Know the rules before you ask

All the major platforms allow you to ask for reviews. What they forbid is trying to control what the review says.

Google's [contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) says businesses may encourage genuine reviews, but must not:

- offer incentives, such as payment, discounts or free services, in exchange for a review;
- discourage negative reviews, or selectively ask for reviews only from guests likely to be positive;
- require or pressure guests to leave ratings while they are on the premises;
- ask for specific content to be included.

Airbnb's [reviews policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/548) says members may not coerce, intimidate, extort, threaten, incentivise or manipulate another person to influence a review.

Two habits follow from these rules:

1. **Ask every guest, the same way.** Do not send the Google link only to the guests who smiled at check-out. That is the "selective" asking Google prohibits.
2. **Never trade anything for a review.** No "10% off your next stay if you leave five stars", and no free breakfast for a review.

## Ask at the right moment

Timing matters more than wording.

- **On check-out day,** the stay is fresh. A short message a few hours after check-out works well.
- **Airbnb gives guests 14 days after check-out to write a review,** according to the same help article, so there is no need to message Airbnb guests about it yourself.
- **One reminder is enough.** If a guest has not responded to a polite request and one follow-up, let it go.

## Make it one tap

Every extra step loses reviewers. Google lets you [share a direct review link or QR code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en) from your Business Profile: open "Read reviews", then "Get more reviews". Use it in three places:

- **Your check-out message** on WhatsApp or email.
- **A small printed card** at reception or in the room, with the QR code.
- **Your receipt or invoice.**

## A message you can adapt

> Hi [Name], thank you for staying with us at [property name]. We hope the journey to [next stop] is going well. If you have a minute, we'd be grateful for an honest review on Google: [link]. And if anything wasn't right, please tell me directly too. It helps us improve. Safe travels, [your name]

Note what this does: it asks for an honest review, gives the link to every guest, and invites private feedback as well, not instead.

## Private feedback without gatekeeping

Hearing about a problem privately is useful. A broken shower or a noisy fan is better fixed this week than mentioned in a review next month. You can invite private feedback, but do not use it as a filter that decides who gets the Google link. Send the public review link to everyone, and treat private feedback as an extra channel.

## Earn better reviews, not just more

The surest way to better reviews is to fix the things guests mention. Read your last 20 reviews and note anything mentioned more than once. Many will be things you can act on quickly, for example hot water, Wi-Fi strength, mosquito nets, directions at night or breakfast timing.

Good photos also help, because guests who know what to expect are less likely to be disappointed. See [photos that sell rooms](/blog/photos-that-sell-rooms).

## Reply to every review

Google's advice on [replying to reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) works for every platform: be brief, polite and specific, do not use replies to advertise, and for a negative review acknowledge the problem, apologise where it is fair and offer to continue the conversation privately. A calm, helpful reply to a bad review can reassure future guests more than another five-star rating.

## Checklist

- [ ] Get your Google review link and QR code from your Business Profile.
- [ ] Write one check-out message that asks every guest for an honest review.
- [ ] Print the QR code on a small card for reception.
- [ ] Remove any wording that offers a reward for reviews.
- [ ] Send the same request to every guest, with at most one reminder.
- [ ] Read recent reviews monthly and list repeated complaints.
- [ ] Reply to every new review within a few days.
