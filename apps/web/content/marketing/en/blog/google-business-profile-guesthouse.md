When a past guest, a driver's customer or a traveller in Galle searches your property's name, Google usually shows your Business Profile before anything else. If that profile is incomplete, or its "website" button points to Booking.com, you have just paid commission on a guest who was already looking for you. This guide covers setting up the profile, pointing it at your own booking page, and handling reviews.

## Are you eligible?

Google's rules say a business must [make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en) to qualify for a Business Profile. The same page lists "rental or for-sale properties such as vacation homes" as ineligible.

For most guesthouses, homestays and staffed villas this is fine: someone welcomes guests and is on site. If you rent out a self-catering villa with nobody there, a profile for the villa itself may not be allowed, though an office that handles the rentals can be. Read the eligibility page before you create one.

## Get the basics right

Google's [business information guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) are strict and worth following exactly:

- **Name:** your real-world name, as on your sign. No "Best Beach Villa Mirissa – Book Now".
- **Address and map pin:** check the pin is on your gate, not the main road. Guests arriving at night will follow it.
- **Category:** choose the closest lodging category, such as guest house, bed and breakfast or hotel.
- **Website:** a site that represents your property. Your own booking site, not an OTA listing.
- **Description:** plain text; links are not allowed in the description.
- **Photos:** add your own recent photos of rooms, the view and the entrance. Our guide to [photos that sell rooms](/blog/photos-that-sell-rooms) covers what to shoot.

## Point the profile at your own booking page

The website field is the most valuable link you have. Set it to your own booking site, ideally the page where guests choose dates.

Depending on your category and country, Google may also show a bookings section where you can add a booking link. If the option appears for your profile, add your own booking page there too.

### What about the prices Google shows?

For hotels, Google can show room prices from booking sites in a booking module. These come from Google's hotel programme: partners connected through Hotel Center can appear there as [free booking links](https://support.google.com/hotelprices/answer/10472394?hl=en), which cost them nothing. Since 2022, individual hotels that are verified and have online booking on their website have also been able to [enter their own rates through the Business Profile](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/hotel-booking-links-now-available-in-google-search-maps/443004/). Whether this is offered to your property depends on Google's current eligibility rules, so check what your profile shows rather than assuming.

## Add a WhatsApp link

Many guests would rather message than fill in a form. WhatsApp's help centre explains [how to link to your WhatsApp from your Google Business Profile](https://faq.whatsapp.com/712818078160617). A [click-to-chat link](https://faq.whatsapp.com/5913398998672934) with pre-filled text such as "Hi, do you have a room for…" makes the first message easy. Then reply quickly and clearly, with your booking link in the first answer.

## Reviews: ask everyone, the same way

Reviews appear next to your profile on Google Search and Maps, and they help guests decide. Google lets you [share a review link or QR code](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en): open your profile, go to "Read reviews", then "Get more reviews", and share or save the code. Print the QR for reception and add the link to your check-out message.

Google's [contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en) sets the limits. You may encourage genuine reviews, but you must not:

- offer payment, discounts or free services in exchange for a review;
- discourage negative reviews, or selectively ask only happy guests;
- pressure guests to leave a rating while they are on your premises;
- ask for specific content to be included.

The safest habit is to send the same short request to every guest after check-out. Our guide to [getting more guest reviews](/blog/getting-more-reviews) goes further.

## Reply to reviews

Google's advice on [replying to reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en) is sensible: keep replies short, polite and specific; do not advertise in replies; and for a negative review, acknowledge what went wrong, apologise where it is fair, and offer to continue privately. Future guests read your replies as closely as the reviews.

## Keep it current

- Update photos once a season.
- Mark temporary closures, for example during renovation or the monsoon.
- Post a short update when something changes: a new room, a new pool, or airport pickup.
- Check the map pin and phone number twice a year.

## Checklist

- [ ] Confirm your property is eligible under Google's guidelines.
- [ ] Use your real name, correct category and an accurate map pin.
- [ ] Set the website to your own booking page, not an OTA.
- [ ] Add a booking link if the option appears for your category.
- [ ] Add a WhatsApp click-to-chat link.
- [ ] Print your review QR code for reception.
- [ ] Ask every guest for a review in the same way, with no incentives.
- [ ] Reply to every review, briefly and politely.
