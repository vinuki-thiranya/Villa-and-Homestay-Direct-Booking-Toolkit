Guests choose with their eyes first. Before they read your description or compare prices, they scroll through your photos. You do not need a professional photographer or an expensive camera; a recent phone, good light and a little preparation will do. This guide covers the preparation, the technique and a shot list you can work through in a morning.

## Prepare the room before the camera

Most bad room photos are bad because of the room, not the camera.

- **Clean and clear.** Remove bags, chargers, bins, toiletries and anything on the floor. Airbnb's own photo advice starts with this: [keep the space clean and free of clutter](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/746).
- **Make the bed properly.** Smooth sheets, plumped pillows, and a folded towel or a frangipani flower is enough styling.
- **Open everything.** Curtains, windows and doors to the balcony.
- **Fix small things.** A missing bulb, a crooked picture or a torn mosquito net will be in the photo.
- **Wipe the lens.** A phone that lives in a pocket usually has a smudged lens.

## Light is everything

Airbnb's [photo guide](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/how-to-get-more-bookings-14) gives three simple rules that suit Sri Lanka well:

- **Turn the flash off** and use daylight from windows and doors.
- **Shoot indoors when the room gets the most daylight,** and only switch on lamps if the room is still too dark.
- **Shoot outdoors in the hour after sunrise or before sunset,** when the light is warm and soft.

Midday sun on the coast is harsh. It burns out white walls and makes deep shadows under palms. Plan pool, garden and view shots for early morning or late afternoon, and photograph interiors in between.

## Technique with a phone

- **Hold the phone horizontally.** Airbnb notes that photos are [cropped into squares in search results and shown in landscape on listing pages](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/how-to-get-more-bookings-14).
- **Turn on the grid** and keep vertical lines straight: door frames and walls should not lean.
- **Hold the phone level** at about chest height, not tilted up or down.
- **Shoot from a corner** to show the size of the room, standing in the doorway if the room is small.
- **Be careful with the ultra-wide lens.** It shows more of the room but stretches the edges. Use it for small bathrooms; use the normal lens where you can.
- **Use HDR** if your phone has it, so the bright window and the darker room both look natural.
- **Keep files large.** Airbnb asks for at least [1024 × 683 pixels](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/746) and suggests larger is better. Any recent phone is well above that; just do not send photos to yourself through a messaging app, which can compress them.

## Be honest

Guests compare your photos with the room when they arrive. Photos that flatter too much can lead to disappointed guests and unhappy reviews. Do not use photos of a different room, an old renovation, or a view only visible from the roof. Show what each room type really looks like, and caption shared bathrooms clearly. If you have helpful accessibility features, such as step-free access or wide doors, photograph those too, as [Airbnb also recommends](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/746).

## A shot list for a morning

Work through this list for each room type. Airbnb suggests a mix of wide, mid-range and close-up shots for every space guests can use.

**For each room type:**

- a wide shot from the corner showing the bed and the window;
- the view from the bed or the balcony;
- the bathroom, wide;
- one or two details: the desk, a reading chair, local craftwork.

**For the property:**

- the front entrance as guests arrive, which helps at night;
- the pool, garden, terrace or rooftop;
- breakfast as you actually serve it;
- shared areas such as the lounge or kitchen;
- the view, beach or paddy fields nearby;
- your sign, so guests know they have arrived.

## Order and captions

Your first photo is the one guests see first, so make it count. Lead with your strongest image, usually the view, the pool or your best room, then follow a guest's path: arrival, common areas, rooms, bathrooms, extras.

Where a platform allows captions, write a short one for each photo, such as "Garden double, ground floor, sleeps 2". Captions answer questions before guests have to ask them, which saves you WhatsApp time.

Use the same set of photos on your own website, your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-guesthouse), Booking.com and Airbnb, so guests recognise the place wherever they find you.

## Refresh once a season

Repaint, new cushions, a new plunge pool: update the photos. Also refresh them when a recent review mentions something that has changed. Our guide to [getting more guest reviews](/blog/getting-more-reviews) shows how to use review feedback this way.

## Checklist

- [ ] Clean, clear and fix each room before shooting.
- [ ] Wipe the lens; turn off the flash; turn on the grid.
- [ ] Shoot interiors in daylight and outdoors near sunrise or sunset.
- [ ] Hold the phone horizontally, level, from the corner.
- [ ] Complete the shot list for every room type and the property.
- [ ] Put your strongest photo first and caption photos where the platform allows.
- [ ] Use the same photos on every platform.
- [ ] Re-shoot after any change to a room.
