A guest who stays for a month fills 30 nights with one check-in, one clean at the end and one set of questions. For a small guesthouse, especially in the quiet months, that can be worth more than a run of two-night stays. Sri Lanka's new Digital Nomad Visa makes this kind of guest more common. This guide covers what the visa means for hosts, how to price a monthly stay, and what paperwork long-stay guests need from you.

## The Digital Nomad Visa in brief

Sri Lanka launched its Digital Nomad Visa in [February 2026](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Govt-launches-Digital-Nomad-Visas-to-professionals/44-788157). According to that Daily FT report:

- it is valid for up to one year and can be renewed each year;
- the main applicant needs a minimum monthly remittance of USD 2,000, plus USD 500 a month for each dependant beyond two;
- the fee is USD 500 a year for the main applicant, spouse and each dependant;
- it is for people aged 18 or over who work remotely, freelance or run a business registered outside Sri Lanka;
- applications go through the Department of Immigration and Emigration.

The first visas were [issued later that month](https://www.ft.lk/front-page/Sri-Lanka-issues-first-Digital-Nomad-Visa/44-788961). For hosts, the point is simple: some guests can now stay legally for a year, not a few weeks, and they need somewhere to live and work.

Visa rules change, and you are not the guest's adviser. If a guest asks what documents they need, point them to the Department of Immigration and Emigration.

## What long-stay guests look for

A monthly guest judges your property differently from a holidaymaker.

- **Reliable internet.** Run a speed test in the room, not at reception, and share the result. Have a backup, such as a mobile data router, for outages.
- **Somewhere to work.** A proper desk and a chair you can sit on for six hours matter more than a sea view from the bed.
- **Power.** Mention any backup power for cuts.
- **Quiet during the day,** and a fair house rule about noise.
- **A kitchen or fridge,** or a clear arrangement for meals.
- **Laundry,** included or at a fixed price.

## How to price a monthly stay

Both big platforms treat 28 nights as "monthly". Airbnb lets hosts set a [monthly discount for stays of 28 nights or more](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1233), and Booking.com introduced [weekly (7-night) and monthly (28-night) rate plans](https://news.booking.com/bookingcom-introduces-long-stay-rates-to-cater-to-the-evolving-travel-demand/) in 2020. Using the same thresholds on your own site keeps things simple.

To set the discount, start from what a long stay saves you, not from a round number.

### Example: one room, 30 nights, in the quiet season

This is an illustration with assumed figures. Use your own costs.

| Item | Nightly guests | One monthly guest |
|---|---|---|
| Nights sold (assumed) | 15 | 30 |
| Rate | LKR 12,000 a night | LKR 252,000 a month (30% off) |
| Room revenue | LKR 180,000 | LKR 252,000 |
| Turnover cleans (assumed) | 6 | 1, plus a weekly clean |
| Booking commission (assumed 15% on OTA nights) | LKR 27,000 | none if booked direct |

In this example the monthly guest earns more, needs fewer changeovers and pays no commission, even at 30% off. In peak season the sums change: a room that would sell every night at a high rate may not need a monthly discount at all. ## Utilities: included or metered

Air conditioning running all day can cost far more over a month than a holidaymaker's few nights. Decide in advance:

- **Included:** simplest for the guest; set a fair limit or price it in.
- **Metered:** record the meter reading at check-in and check-out and charge at a stated rate per unit.

Write the choice into the booking confirmation, along with what is included: cleaning frequency, linen changes, water, Wi-Fi and laundry.

## Deposits and payment

For long stays, take a larger deposit and payment in advance:

- **First month in advance,** or a deposit plus the balance before arrival;
- **a refundable security deposit** if you include utilities or the room has expensive equipment;
- **the following months paid before each month starts.**

Bank transfer often suits long stays, because the amounts are large and card fees add up. PayPal charges [4.40% plus a fixed fee on international payments](https://www.paypal.com/lk/webapps/mpp/merchant-fees), so on a month's rent the difference matters. Our guide to [taking deposits](/blog/deposits-payhere-lankaqr) compares the options.

For stays of several months, a short written agreement covering dates, price, utilities, notice period and house rules avoids misunderstandings. For anything that looks like a lease, take local legal advice.

## Invoices that do the job

A long-stay guest will often need proof of where they live and what they paid, whether for their employer, their own accounts or official paperwork. A good invoice includes:

- your property's name, address and contact details;
- your SLTDA registration number, if registered (see [SLTDA registration basics](/blog/sltda-registration-basics));
- the guest's full name as on their passport;
- stay dates and the period each payment covers;
- amounts paid, currency and payment method;
- utilities or extras shown as separate lines;
- an invoice number and issue date.

Issue one invoice per payment period, and never edit an invoice once issued. If something changes, issue a correction.

## Checklist

- [ ] Test Wi-Fi in each long-stay room and arrange a backup.
- [ ] Set up a desk and work chair in at least one room.
- [ ] Decide your weekly (7+ nights) and monthly (28+ nights) discounts.
- [ ] Choose whether utilities are included or metered, and write it down.
- [ ] Set deposit and monthly payment rules for long stays.
- [ ] Prepare a short written agreement for stays of several months.
- [ ] Make sure your invoices include every item in the list above.
