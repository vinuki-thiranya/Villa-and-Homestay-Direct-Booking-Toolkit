If you host tourists in Sri Lanka, registering with the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA) is not optional, and enforcement has become more visible. This guide explains the basics: which category you might fall into, what the process looks like, the documents SLTDA asks for, and what it costs. It is a starting point, not legal advice. SLTDA's own documents are the final word, and they change, so check the current versions before you apply.

## Why registration matters

Registration of tourism service providers is a [mandatory requirement under the Tourism Act](https://www.ft.lk/Front-Page/Sri-Lanka-Tourism-deploys-cyber-tool-to-track-accommodation-providers-sans-valid-license/44-652944), and SLTDA has long compared online listings against its own register to find unlicensed properties.

In February 2025, SLTDA's chairman said owners had been told that [displaying a property online without SLTDA registration is an offence](https://www.sundaytimes.lk/250216/business-times/sri-lanka-tourism-to-raid-illegal-lodgings-587541.html). The same report estimated 16,000 to 20,000 unregistered properties listed online, described raids in Mirissa and Unawatuna, and mentioned a plan to encourage tourists to ask for a property's registration number.

Beyond avoiding trouble, registration helps your direct bookings. A guest paying a deposit to a property they found on Google has less protection than one booking through an app, and a registration number on your website and invoices is one more reason to trust you. If SLTDA's campaign to get tourists asking for registration numbers takes hold, you will want yours ready to show.

## Which category are you?

SLTDA's [Register with us](https://www.sltda.gov.lk/register-with-us) page lists separate accommodation categories, each with its own process and document list. Those most relevant to small properties include:

- **Homestays** — up to four bedrooms;
- **Guest houses**;
- **Bungalows**, and heritage homes and bungalows;
- **Rented homes** and **rented apartments**;
- **Boutique villas** — at least five rooms.

The right category depends on the number of rooms and how the property is run, and each category has its own minimum requirements. If you are unsure, call SLTDA before you apply rather than guessing.

## The process, step by step

SLTDA's homestay and guest house guides describe the same four stages. This summary follows the [homestay document](https://sltda.gov.lk/storage/common_media/home-stay-units-F3795177392.pdf) and the [guest house document](https://sltda.gov.lk/storage/common_media/Document%20Requirement-Guest%20House.pdf).

1. **Online application.** Apply through SLTDA's [online portal](https://eservices.sltda.gov.lk/ApplicationMain.aspx) under your category. SLTDA confirms the application and gives you a login.
2. **Admin payment.** Pay the first fee online or at a bank, uploading the slip. SLTDA says validation takes about two days, and you are told by email and SMS.
3. **Inspection and documents.** SLTDA inspects the property. Once your documents are accepted, you send hard copies certified as true copies by an attorney-at-law or a Justice of the Peace.
4. **Registration and licence.** If the property meets the gazetted minimum requirements, you pay the registration fee and collect your licence and registration certificate. If not, SLTDA tells you what to fix.

## Documents you may need

The lists differ by category. As examples:

**Homestay** (from SLTDA's homestay document):

- deed or lease agreement;
- no-objection letter from the owner of the premises;
- no-objection letter from the police, or a Grama Niladhari certificate;
- true copies of the documents.

**Guest house** (from SLTDA's guest house document):

- business registration;
- approved building plan, or a consent letter from the local authority or UDA;
- environmental clearance or consent letter;
- public liability insurance;
- workmen's compensation insurance;
- certificate of fire protection;
- trade licence and deed or lease, listed as optional.

Start collecting these early. Several come from other offices, such as the local authority or the police, so allow time for each.

## What it costs

These figures come from SLTDA's published documents. The homestay document dates from 2019, so check the current fees before you pay.

| Category | Registration total (LKR) | Yearly renewal (LKR) |
|---|---|---|
| Homestay | 8,734.69 | 6,551.02 |
| Guest house, C grade or B&B, 1–10 rooms | 30,982.56 | 16,459.49 |
| Guest house, A grade, 1–10 rooms | 33,887.18 | 19,364.10 |

The guest house fees include SSCL and VAT, according to the document. A name or ownership change is listed at LKR 2,000.

## Renewal

The guest house document gives a renewal fee per year, and both documents list what to send for renewal, including a fresh police or Grama Niladhari letter for homestays and current insurance and fire certificates for guest houses. SLTDA's registration page says renewal payments must be made online. Put the expiry date in your calendar with a month's warning.

## Checklist

- [ ] Read SLTDA's page for your category and download the latest document list.
- [ ] Phone SLTDA if you are unsure which category fits.
- [ ] Collect deeds, letters, insurance and certificates before applying.
- [ ] Apply online and pay the admin fee.
- [ ] Prepare the property for inspection against the minimum requirements.
- [ ] Get true copies certified by an attorney-at-law or JP.
- [ ] Show your SLTDA number on your website, receipts and invoices.
- [ ] Set a reminder a month before renewal.
