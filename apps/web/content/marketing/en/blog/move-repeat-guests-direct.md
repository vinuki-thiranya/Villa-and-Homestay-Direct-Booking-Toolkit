The cheapest booking you will ever get is from someone who has already stayed with you. You know they like the place, they know you, and there is no reason for a platform to take a cut the second time. The difficulty is doing this without breaking the rules of the platform that introduced you, which can cost you your listing.

This guide separates what is clearly fine, what needs care, and what to avoid.

## Why repeat and referral guests are the right target

A first-time guest from Europe probably found you on Booking.com or Airbnb, and the commission on that booking paid for that introduction. Our [Booking.com commission maths](/blog/booking-com-commission-maths) shows how quickly those fees add up.

A repeat guest, or a friend sent by a past guest, is different. The platform did not find them this time; you did, by giving them a good stay. These are the bookings to bring home first. They are also the guests most likely to pay a deposit directly, because they already trust you.

## Know the platform rules first

The rules differ by platform and sometimes by region. Read the current version for your own account; the summaries below are what we found at the time of writing.

### Airbnb

Airbnb's [Off-Platform and Fee Transparency Policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2799) says guests and hosts may not:

- ask each other to communicate off Airbnb before a booking is made;
- put links in listings or messages that take people off Airbnb;
- offer or ask for discounts to book off Airbnb;
- cancel a reservation in order to rebook it elsewhere.

The same policy says payments for "current, future, or repeat bookings" must stay on Airbnb. In practice, treat Airbnb guests carefully: do not message them through Airbnb about booking direct, and do not hand them a "book direct for 10% off" card during their stay. If an Airbnb guest later finds your website or Google profile on their own and books there, that is their choice; actively steering them is the risk.

### Booking.com

In the European Economic Area, Booking.com [removed parity clauses from its terms in July 2024](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/factsheet-how-dma-ensures-businesses-using-bookingcom-are-free-set-their-prices-and-bookingcom-2026-09-28_en) under the EU Digital Markets Act. Sri Lanka is outside the EEA, so do not assume the same freedom applies to you. Before you publish lower prices on your own site, or message Booking.com guests about future stays, read your General Delivery Terms in the extranet, especially the parts on rate parity and on using guest contact details. If something is unclear, ask Booking.com partner support in writing.

## What is clearly fine

These channels belong to you, so you can promote direct booking through them freely:

- **Your own website and Google Business Profile.** People who search your name should land on your booking page. See our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-guesthouse).
- **Guests who came to you directly.** Walk-ins, WhatsApp enquiries, referrals and anyone who booked on your site can be offered a returning-guest rate.
- **Signage that is not aimed at one platform's guests.** Your website address on the gate, the menu or the Wi-Fi card is ordinary branding.
- **Partners.** Drivers, surf schools and tour guides who send you guests can share your booking link.

## Building a returning-guest list the right way

A returning-guest list is only useful if people agreed to join it.

1. **Ask for consent clearly.** At check-in or check-out, ask direct guests whether they would like to hear about returning-guest offers, and note their answer. A simple yes or no on your registration form is enough.
2. **Store what you need, nothing more.** Name, country, WhatsApp or email, dates stayed and any preferences, such as the ground-floor room or a vegetarian breakfast.
3. **Respect "no".** Guests from the EU are covered by GDPR, and every guest deserves a way to unsubscribe.
4. **Keep it personal.** "Hi Anna, the front room is free for the second week of February; returning guests get 10% off" works better than a newsletter.

## Make the direct route easier than the app

If booking direct is harder than tapping "reserve" in an app, people will use the app. Remove the friction:

- **Live availability** on your site, not "message us to check".
- **A clear price in the guest's currency,** with the deposit and cancellation policy shown before payment.
- **A payment method they already use:** card, PayPal, UPI via LankaQR for Indian guests, or bank transfer.
- **Fast replies on WhatsApp.** Our guide to [WhatsApp messages that turn enquiries into bookings](/blog/whatsapp-messages-that-convert) has templates.
- **A returning-guest code** that works on your site, sent privately to people on your list.

## Timing the reminder

A message the week after a guest leaves is polite, but it rarely arrives when they are planning their next trip. A message around 10 to 11 months after their stay arrives at about the time of year they last travelled, which is a better moment to remind them you exist. For seasonal places such as the south coast or Arugam Bay, send it before the season's prices are set on the OTAs.

## Checklist

- [ ] Read Airbnb's off-platform policy and your Booking.com General Delivery Terms.
- [ ] Decide which guests you will approach: direct, walk-in and referral guests only.
- [ ] Add a consent question to your check-in form.
- [ ] Put your booking link on your Google Business Profile, gate sign and Wi-Fi card.
- [ ] Create a returning-guest code with a fixed percentage and an end date.
- [ ] Schedule a reminder to each consenting guest about 11 months after their stay.
- [ ] Give drivers and partners your booking link.
- [ ] Ask every guest for an honest review too.
