Sri Lanka's tourist calendar has an unusual twist: when one coast is wet, the other is usually drier. For a guesthouse in Mirissa or a surf camp in Arugam Bay, that means the year divides into very different halves. This guide explains the seasons, then turns them into a practical rate plan.

## Two monsoons, four seasons

Sri Lanka Tourism's [climate guide](https://www.srilanka.travel/weather) describes two monsoons and four seasons:

| Months | Season | What it means for hosts |
|---|---|---|
| December–February | North-east monsoon | Peak season on the south-west coast; clear skies and calm sea there |
| March–April | First inter-monsoon | Transitional, with afternoon thunderstorms |
| May–September | South-west monsoon | Heavy rain on the south-west coast and hills; the guide recommends this period for the dry zone, including Trincomalee and Arugam Bay |
| October–November | Second inter-monsoon | Occasional heavy showers across the island |

For the east, independent climate data adds detail: on the east coast, [rain is heaviest from about October to January, while June to August is sunnier there](https://www.climatestotravel.com/climate/sri-lanka) and sheltered from the south-west monsoon.

The short version:

- **South and west coast** (Bentota to Tangalle): peak from December to February, wet and quiet from May to September.
- **East coast** (Arugam Bay, Pasikudah, Trincomalee): the drier months fall in the middle of the year, and the heaviest rain at the end of it.
- **Hill country** (Ella, Kandy, Nuwara Eliya): the south-west monsoon brings rain to the hills too, so plan for a wetter middle of the year.

Weather is not the only driver. School holidays in source countries, Christmas and New Year, and Sri Lankan public holidays all move demand. Your own booking history is the best guide; use the seasons as the frame and your data to fill it in.

## Turn the seasons into rate periods

For a small property, three or four periods are usually easier to manage than a different price every week.

### Example: a south-coast guesthouse

This is an illustration, not a recommendation. Your rates depend on your rooms, location and reviews.

**Assumption:** a base double-room rate of LKR 15,000 a night.

| Period | Dates (example) | Rate | Minimum stay | Deposit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peak | 20 Dec – 10 Jan | LKR 24,000 | 4 nights | 50%, non-refundable |
| High | 11 Jan – 15 Apr | LKR 18,000 | 2 nights | 30%, moderate policy |
| Shoulder | 16 Apr – 31 May, Oct – 19 Dec | LKR 15,000 | 1 night | 30%, flexible |
| Monsoon | June – September | LKR 11,000 | 1 night | 30%, flexible; long-stay discounts |

An east-coast property would turn this around, with its high period in the middle of the year and its quiet period at the end.

## Tactics for the busy months

- **Set rates early.** If your OTA rates for next January are still last year's, anyone booking early is getting last year's price.
- **Use minimum stays** around Christmas and New Year, so a single-night booking does not block a week.
- **Ask for a larger deposit** with a stricter cancellation policy. You can resell a cancelled room in February; you may not be able to in June. Our guide to [taking deposits](/blog/deposits-payhere-lankaqr) explains the options.
- **Watch for gaps.** A two-night gap between bookings is hard to sell; a small discount for exactly those nights often fills it.

## Tactics for the quiet months

Quiet does not mean empty. Different guests travel in the off-season:

- **Long stays.** A remote worker staying a month may care more about a fair monthly price and good Wi-Fi than about peak-season weather. Weekly and monthly rates can fill the calendar. See [long stays and digital nomads](/blog/digital-nomad-long-stays).
- **Domestic travellers,** such as weekend visitors from Colombo, if your location suits a short break.
- **Last-minute discounts** for bookings made within a few days of arrival.
- **Packages** that add value rather than cutting price, such as breakfast plus a cooking class or a day trip to the dry zone.

It is often better to add value than to keep dropping the nightly rate. Very low prices set expectations that can be hard to undo next year.

## Keep every channel consistent

Rate changes need to appear on Booking.com, Airbnb and your own site. iCal sync only shares availability, not prices, so update rates on each platform yourself, or use a system that pushes them. Our [iCal sync guide](/blog/ical-sync-double-bookings) explains what is and is not synced.

Keep an eye on commission too. Commission is a percentage, so every peak-season booking through an OTA costs you more in rupees than the same nights in the monsoon.

## Checklist

- [ ] Mark your peak, high, shoulder and quiet periods for the next 12 months.
- [ ] Set a rate, minimum stay and deposit rule for each period.
- [ ] Update rates on every OTA and on your own site before guests start booking.
- [ ] Add weekly and monthly discounts for the quiet months.
- [ ] Note Christmas, New Year and local holidays as special dates.
- [ ] Review last year's bookings by month to adjust the periods.
- [ ] Plan one or two off-season packages that add value.
